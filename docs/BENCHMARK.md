# Benchmark: settling a dispute amicably in France

What already exists for someone with an everyday dispute (neighbours, purchase, rented home,
money, family), what is missing, and where this project stands. Written during the night work
(#80, October 2026). Update it when the project or the landscape changes.

## Existing services

| Service | What it does well | What is missing for our goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **justice.fr** (Ministry of Justice) | Authoritative fact sheets: conciliator, civil mediator, consumer mediation… | One page per route, legal vocabulary, no comparison between routes, no help to choose, no practice |
| **service-public.gouv.fr** | Exhaustive procedures, costs, delays, forms | Administrative tone; you must already know what you are looking for |
| **conciliateurs.fr** | Finds a conciliator near you, online request | Says *where*, not *whether* conciliation suits your case or how to prepare |
| **economie.gouv.fr (DGCCRF), médiation de la consommation** | Clear rules (written complaint, free, 90 days), list of mediators | Only consumer disputes |
| **Caf, médiation familiale** | Income-based scale, local services | Only family disputes |
| **Private online platforms** (online dispute resolution, online court filing) | Fast, digital, sometimes free to start | Often a paid service; some steer users towards court; not neutral information |
| **Information points** (points-justice, maisons de justice et du droit) | Free legal information with a person | Opening hours and travel; people go there once the conflict has escalated |

## What nobody does (our angle)

1. **Orientation from the person's situation**: « J’ai un conflit ? » asks a few questions and points to the right amicable route. It says when the law makes the attempt **mandatory** (art. 750-1 CPC: claims of 5,000 € or less, neighbourhood disturbances), a rule most people discover only when their claim is refused.
2. **Comparison in plain words**: talking, conciliation, mediation, consumer mediation and the judge, side by side, on the same criteria: who helps, cost, who decides, duration, what the outcome is worth (`/demarches`).
3. **Practice before the real situation**:
   - role-play scenarios with levels, from three points of view (the person who suffers the trouble, the person accused of it, the mediator);
   - mini-games on wording (non-violent communication) and on who does what.

   Official sites inform; nobody trains.
4. **Concrete, information-only tools**: a preparation checklist and, for each third party, what the law says, its cost and the value of the agreement. The site informs; it never writes or sends anything for the user.
5. **Accessible and private by design**: RGAA self-assessment, every journey state scanned by axe; nothing is sent or stored on a server, and the scores stay on the device.
6. **IRIS**, a chatbot that answers only from the site's sourced content, gives its sources and says when it does not know.

## Key figures (sourced)

- 189,790 requests to conciliators of justice in 2023, 48 % of direct requests ending in an agreement, 2,888 volunteer conciliators: Ministère de la Justice, *Infostat Justice* n° 201, August 2025.
- Conciliation is free; the consumer mediator is free for the consumer and must answer within 90 days of admissibility (DGCCRF); family mediation costs between 2 € and 131 € per session depending on income (Caf scale).
- Since 13 May 2023, an amicable attempt is again mandatory before court for claims of 5,000 € or less and for neighbourhood disturbances (art. 750-1 CPC, décret n° 2023-357). Without it, the claim can be declared inadmissible.

The success rate of direct requests to conciliators fell from 57 % (2015) to 48 % (2023) while requests rose (Infostat n° 201). Arriving **prepared and in the right frame of mind** is exactly what the site trains.
